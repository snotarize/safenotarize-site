What Is a Caregiver Authorization Affidavit?

A caregiver authorization affidavit is a written statement made under oath or affirmation about a child’s caregiver arrangement, the signer’s relationship to the child, or permissions described in the document. Some jurisdictions, schools, medical providers, benefits programs, or other recipients publish their own caregiver affidavit or declaration. Other recipients may request a privately prepared affidavit containing specific facts.

An affidavit is different from a simple authorization letter because the signer is swearing or affirming that the stated facts are true. It is also different from a court order. A notarized caregiver affidavit does not automatically appoint a legal guardian, transfer custody, or give every form of parental authority. The document’s effect depends on applicable law and whether the receiving organization accepts it.

SafeNotarize does not prepare the affidavit, choose the statements, determine the caregiver’s legal authority, or advise whether an affidavit is sufficient. Bring the completed form requested by the recipient and leave the signature unsigned when the notarial certificate requires signing under oath in the notary’s presence.

Roles and requirements

Who Signs and How Does It Differ From Other Family Documents?

The form identifies who must sign. It may be the parent, legal guardian, caregiver, or more than one person. The notary verifies the identity of each signer who appears, but does not verify the truth of every underlying fact, determine custody, or decide whether the signer has authority over the child.

A Temporary Guardianship Authorization is generally a permission document describing temporary authority. A Minor Medical Consent focuses on healthcare permission. A caregiver affidavit focuses on sworn facts or declarations. A court-appointed guardianship is created and supervised through a court process, not by the notary.

Prepare carefully

Identification and Preparation

Because names and roles can be similar, identify the parent, child, caregiver, and receiving organization consistently. If the form asks whether the caregiver is a relative, lives with the child, or has written parental consent, answer only according to the signer’s knowledge and the form’s instructions.

Oath, Identification, Witnesses, and Online Notarization

Many affidavits use a jurat, meaning the signer appears before the notary, takes an oath or affirmation, and signs in the required manner. Do not sign early unless the document and notary instruct otherwise. An acknowledgment may be used in some documents instead. The notarial certificate controls the act the notary performs.

The signer must present acceptable government-issued photo identification and complete remote identity verification. Review the acceptable ID guide. Supporting documents about the child, residence, custody, or relationship may be requested by the recipient, but the notary generally does not decide which records prove the underlying authority.

Witness requirements vary. A caregiver affidavit may request witnesses, notarization, both, or neither. If witnesses are required, confirm their qualifications and participation method before the session. Online notarization is available only when the document, applicable law, platform, and receiving organization permit it.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common examples vary by the receiving school, organization, caregiver arrangement, and purpose.

🏫

Using an affidavit when the recipient requires a court order

A sworn statement cannot replace a required custody, guardianship, or court document.

👨‍👩‍👧

Signing before the oath or affirmation

A jurat normally requires the signer to swear or affirm and sign as directed during the notarial act.

📋

Leaving relationships or dates unclear

Identify the child, caregiver, parent or guardian, effective period, and stated permissions precisely.

🧭

Assuming medical consent is automatically included

A healthcare provider may require a separate minor medical consent or statutory surrogate form.

⚠️

Submitting the affidavit without supporting records

The recipient may separately request identification, proof of relationship, school records, residence evidence, or court documents.

Caregiver Authorization Affidavit FAQ

Answers to common preparation and online-notarization questions.

Is a caregiver authorization affidavit a court guardianship order?

No. A notarized affidavit is not a court appointment and does not by itself create court-supervised guardianship.

Who swears to the facts in a caregiver affidavit?

The form identifies the declarant. It may be a parent, guardian, caregiver, or another person with personal knowledge of the stated facts.

Must I sign a caregiver affidavit in front of the notary?

If the document uses a jurat, the signer generally signs as part of the oath or affirmation. Follow the certificate and the notary’s instructions.

Can a caregiver affidavit authorize medical treatment?

Only if applicable law and the receiving provider accept the document for that purpose. A separate medical-consent or surrogate form may be required.

Does the notary verify that the caregiver is related to the child?

The notary verifies the signer’s identity and performs the notarial act. The recipient may request separate evidence of relationship or authority.

Can a caregiver affidavit be used for school enrollment?

Some schools or jurisdictions use caregiver affidavits, while others require custody records, residency evidence, or their own forms. Confirm with the school.

What happens if the caregiver arrangement changes?

Ask the receiving organizations how to update or revoke the affidavit and whether a new document must be signed and notarized.

May two parents sign the same caregiver affidavit separately?

That depends on the form and recipient. Confirm whether both signatures are required and whether separate online sessions are acceptable.
