What an Authorized Signer Affidavit Generally States

An authorized signer affidavit is a sworn or affirmed statement concerning a person’s authority to sign or act for a company, nonprofit, association, partnership, trust, government body, or another organization. The affidavit may identify the entity, the signer’s title or relationship, the transaction, the source or scope of claimed authority, and supporting records requested by the recipient.

Businesses may encounter this document in banking, lending, real estate, contracting, vendor onboarding, licensing, insurance, vehicle, procurement, or government transactions. The recipient may use its own form and may request resolutions, operating agreements, public records, incumbency certificates, powers of attorney, or identification in addition to the affidavit.

SafeNotarize can notarize an eligible signature on a completed affidavit. We do not create the statement, decide what authority exists, verify company records, or determine whether the affidavit is sufficient for the transaction.

Sworn Affidavit Versus Authorization Letter

An affidavit is generally a factual statement made under oath or affirmation. A Business Authorization Letter usually grants or documents permission for a named representative to perform a limited task. The two documents can relate to the same transaction, but they are not interchangeable.

An affidavit may state that the signer is an officer, manager, member, partner, trustee, employee, or authorized representative and may describe the basis for authority. An authorization letter may instead be signed by another business representative to give permission. A Letter of Authorization can also concern personal rather than business authority.

Customers should use the document requested by the receiving organization. Do not replace a resolution, power of attorney, secretary’s certificate, bank form, or agency affidavit with a general authorization letter merely because the language seems similar.

Company Authority Versus Personal Identity

Personal identity and company authority are separate questions. The notary verifies the identity of the individual who appears and performs the requested acknowledgment or jurat. The notary does not verify that the individual owns the company, holds a particular office, was properly appointed, or has authority for the transaction.

The signer should use the exact personal name shown on acceptable identification and the exact legal entity name shown in the prepared affidavit. The representative title or capacity—such as president, manager, member, partner, trustee, or authorized agent—should match the document supplied by the recipient.

Representative-capacity notarial wording may record that the individual acknowledged a document as an officer or other representative for the named entity. That wording describes the stated capacity; it is not a legal determination that the authority is valid.

Supporting Records a Recipient May Request

A receiving organization may separately request a corporate or LLC resolution, operating agreement excerpt, articles, bylaws, annual report, certificate of status, public business record, secretary’s certificate, incumbency certificate, power of attorney, contract, board consent, trust certificate, or specimen signature. The required evidence depends on the entity, transaction, risk level, and recipient policy.

A notarized affidavit does not replace those records unless the recipient agrees. For example, a bank may require both the affidavit and its own authorized-signer form. A title company may request a resolution and proof of the representative’s office. A government agency may use a prescribed affidavit that cannot be replaced with a custom statement.

Collect the requested evidence before notarization. SafeNotarize does not authenticate company records or advise which records prove authority.

Notarial Act, Identification, Witnesses, and Online Eligibility

Because the document is an affidavit, it may require a jurat in which the signer swears or affirms that the statements are true. Do not sign before the session when the jurat requires signing in the notary’s presence. Some forms instead request an acknowledgment. The prepared document or recipient should identify the appropriate act.

The signer must complete identity verification and present acceptable government-issued photo identification. Review the acceptable ID guide for name matching, passports, expiration, and technology preparation.

Witnesses are not automatically required for every affidavit. The document, law, and recipient determine whether witnesses participate. Review the witness service guide when another participant is requested. Remote online notarization availability also depends on the document, signer, jurisdiction, platform, and receiving organization.

Common Mistakes and Acceptance Concerns

Common mistakes include signing before taking the oath, leaving authority statements incomplete, confusing a personal title with company authority, using a trade name instead of the legal entity name, omitting the transaction, attaching outdated records, or stating conclusions the signer cannot personally affirm.

Another risk is document substitution. A recipient asking for a corporate resolution, LLC resolution, Secretary’s Certificate, or limited power of attorney may reject a general affidavit. Compare the Corporate Resolution, LLC Member Resolution, Secretary’s Certificate, and Limited Power of Attorney guides.

Customers signing from outside the United States should confirm foreign and receiving-party acceptance through the international online notary guide. Notarization and acceptance are separate.

Authorized Signer Affidavit Notary FAQ

Answers to common preparation and online-notarization questions.

Can an authorized signer affidavit be notarized online?

It may be notarized online when the completed affidavit, signer, requested jurat or acknowledgment, platform, applicable law, and recipient support remote online notarization.

Does notarization prove that I am authorized to sign for the company?

No. Notarization verifies the appearing individual and requested notarial act. The recipient determines whether the affidavit and supporting records establish sufficient authority.

How is this affidavit different from a business authorization letter?

The affidavit is a sworn or affirmed factual statement. A business authorization letter generally grants or records limited permission and may not require an oath.

What supporting documents may accompany an authorized signer affidavit?

A recipient may request resolutions, operating agreements, bylaws, public filings, incumbency certificates, powers of attorney, contracts, or its own authority forms.

Can a contractor or employee sign the affidavit for a business?

Only when the person can truthfully make the requested statements and the business and recipient accept that role. Employment or contractor status alone does not prove authority.

Should I sign an authorized signer affidavit before the notary session?

Not when the affidavit requires a jurat and live oath or affirmation. Leave the signature blank until the notary directs you, unless the recipient confirms another process.

Can the affidavit use a trade name instead of the legal company name?

Use the exact names requested by the recipient. A trade name may need to appear with the legal entity name rather than replacing it.

Will an authorized signer affidavit replace a corporate resolution?

Not automatically. The documents serve different purposes, and a recipient may require the resolution, affidavit, or both. Use the requested instrument.
