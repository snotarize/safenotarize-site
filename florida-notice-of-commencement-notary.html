What a Florida Notice of Commencement Is

A Notice of Commencement is a Florida construction document used within the state’s Construction Lien Law. It provides public information about an improvement to real property, including the property description, owner or lessee, contractor, surety or lender when applicable, designated recipients for notices, and the expected duration of the notice.

Florida Statutes section 713.13 contains a statutory form and states that an owner, lessee, or authorized agent may be responsible for recording the notice before work begins in covered situations. The form also includes warnings about payments, expiration, and project administration. SafeNotarize can notarize an eligible completed Notice of Commencement; we do not decide whether a notice is legally required, select the project parties, or record it with the county.

Use the document approved by the county, contractor, lender, title company, or qualified adviser rather than an old form from another project.

When This Document Is Commonly Used

People commonly encounter a Notice of Commencement when improving Florida real property under a construction contract. Examples include a home addition, roof replacement, major renovation, commercial buildout, pool installation, substantial repair, or another permitted project. The project value, type of work, exemptions, and local permit process may affect the required steps.

The notice is not a construction contract, building permit, lien release, or proof that every contractor and supplier has been paid. It is one document in a larger process. A building department may request recording information before inspections, and the job site may need a posted copy. Those operational steps are controlled by Florida law and the local authority, not by the notary.

If a project has already started, was abandoned, changed contractors, or needs its effective period extended, do not assume a new signature on the original form solves the issue. Florida law addresses amendments, recommencement, and termination separately. Confirm the correct next document before booking.

Who Signs and What Information Should Be Ready

The statutory form identifies the signer as the owner or lessee, or an authorized officer, director, partner, manager, or other authorized representative. The appropriate signer depends on ownership, the construction agreement, entity authority, and the instructions of the receiving office. A contractor or permit applicant should not sign for the owner unless valid authority exists.

Before the session, review the complete legal description and project address, the owner’s interest, the fee-simple titleholder if different, contractor details, surety or bond information if applicable, lender information, designated notice recipients, and the expiration date. Blank material fields can delay notarization or create a recording rejection.

When an entity owns the property, the signer should know the entity’s exact legal name and the title or capacity in which the person signs. SafeNotarize verifies identity and witnesses the signature; it does not verify corporate authority, property ownership, or the accuracy of the legal description.

Notarization, Witnesses, and Online Eligibility

The Florida statutory form includes a notarial acknowledgment and currently shows alternatives for physical presence or online notarization. That language supports remote execution in appropriate circumstances, but it does not guarantee that every county, lender, permit office, platform, document version, or project will accept the same workflow.

The signer must complete identity verification and present acceptable government-issued photo identification. The name on the identification should be consistent with the signer name and capacity shown in the document. If the document is already signed, ask whether the notarial certificate and platform rules allow the signer to acknowledge that signature during the live session.

A Notice of Commencement does not ordinarily add document witnesses merely because it is notarized, but another project document or recipient instruction may request witnesses. The applicable document, law, and receiving organization control. Review the online notary with witnesses guide before booking if another signature or witness is expected.

Recording and County Acceptance Considerations

Notarization and recording are separate events. After the signature is notarized, the customer or project professional remains responsible for submitting the document to the correct county recorder, obtaining certified or recorded copies when needed, posting the required copy, and giving the building or permit authority the requested evidence.

County recording offices can reject documents for reasons unrelated to the notarial act, such as an incomplete legal description, missing return address, incorrect margins, omitted preparer information, unpaid recording fees, an expired or altered form, or inconsistent project data. Electronic recordability can also depend on the county’s systems and submission channel.

Before the session, ask the county clerk or recorder, building department, lender, contractor, or title company whether it accepts remote online notarization, an electronically signed original, and a printed copy of the completed electronic record. SafeNotarize does not guarantee recording, permit approval, inspection scheduling, or county acceptance.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using a form from the wrong county or an outdated project file.
  • Entering only a street address when a legal description is requested.
  • Listing a contractor, owner, lender, or titleholder under an incorrect legal name.
  • Leaving the signer’s title or signing capacity blank for an entity-owned property.
  • Signing before the notary when the chosen notarial process requires a live signature.
  • Assuming notarization automatically records or posts the notice.
  • Choosing an expiration date without checking the project contract and statutory instructions.
  • Trying to use an amendment when a new notice or recommencement may be required.

Slow down and compare the completed form with the permit application, deed or property record, construction contract, and recipient checklist. Correction after recording may require a separate amended notice or other procedure.

Florida Notice of Commencement FAQ

Answers to common preparation and online-notarization questions.

Can a Florida Notice of Commencement be notarized online?

The statutory form currently includes an online-notarization option, and an eligible completed form may be notarized remotely. Confirm that the county recorder, permit authority, lender, and other receiving parties accept the electronic document and delivery format.

Who normally signs the Notice of Commencement?

The form may be signed by the owner or lessee, or by an authorized representative signing in a stated capacity. The project documents and receiving office determine who has proper authority.

Does the online notary record the Notice of Commencement?

No. SafeNotarize performs the notarial act. The customer or project professional remains responsible for county recording, certified copies, posting, permit-office delivery, and related fees.

Should the legal description be included before notarization?

The statutory form requests a property description sufficient for identification and generally includes the legal description. Confirm the exact wording with the deed, property record, county form, or title professional before signing.

Can I use the same notice after changing contractors?

Do not assume so. Florida law addresses amendments and contractor changes specifically, and a new Notice of Commencement or Notice of Recommencement may be required. Verify the correct process first.

What happens if the notice expires before the project is finished?

Expiration can affect the construction-lien process and project administration. Ask the contractor, lender, permit office, title company, or qualified adviser whether an amendment, new notice, or other step is appropriate.

Will a printed copy of an electronically notarized notice be recordable?

County systems and recording rules vary. Ask the recorder whether it accepts an electronic original, electronic submission, or a printed copy of the completed electronic record.

Can SafeNotarize fill out the construction form for me?

No. SafeNotarize notarizes an eligible completed document and does not prepare construction forms, choose legal descriptions, calculate deadlines, or advise about lien rights.
