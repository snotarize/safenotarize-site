What an Affidavit of Correction Is

An affidavit of correction—also called a correction affidavit, scrivener’s affidavit, statement of facts, or affidavit of clerical error in some settings—explains a mistake in an existing document. The affidavit normally identifies the original document, quotes or describes the incorrect information, provides the proposed correction, and states why the affiant has personal knowledge.

There is no universal correction affidavit. A county recorder, DMV, bank, insurer, employer, school, court clerk, title company, licensing office, or other organization may require a specific form and may limit what can be corrected without a new document, amended filing, court order, or additional signatures.

SafeNotarize can notarize an eligible completed correction affidavit. We do not decide whether an error is clerical, choose the correction method, rewrite the original document, or guarantee that the custodian will alter its records.

Common Uses and Important Limits

Correction affidavits may be presented for misspelled names, transposed numbers, incorrect dates, address errors, incomplete descriptions, mistaken identifiers, or other factual entries. They can arise in vehicle-title work, real-estate recordings, business records, insurance files, school records, or private agreements.

Not every mistake can be corrected by affidavit. A correction that changes ownership, price, legal rights, parties, property, obligations, or substantive terms may require a new agreement, re-execution, amendment, agency form, or court process. Some government offices publish detailed correction procedures. Florida’s motor-vehicle manual, for example, separates title and lien correction processes rather than treating every error the same.

Ask the receiving organization whether it accepts a correction affidavit, which person must sign, what evidence must accompany it, and whether the original document must be returned, re-recorded, or replaced.

Who Signs and What Should Be Included

The appropriate affiant depends on who made the error and who has personal knowledge. It may be the original signer, preparer, seller, buyer, officer, custodian, employee, recorder, or other authorized person. SafeNotarize does not determine the legally proper affiant.

A useful recipient-approved form often identifies the original document by title, date, parties, recording or account number, and issuing office. It then states the incorrect text, corrected text, reason for the correction, and supporting evidence. The affiant should avoid changing unrelated content.

Review exact spelling, punctuation, numbers, and dates. Attach copies only when permitted and protect sensitive information. If multiple parties signed the original document, ask whether all must approve or sign the correction.

Identification, Witnesses, and Remote Notarization

The signer must present acceptable government-issued photo identification and complete the platform’s identity process. Names on the affidavit, original record, and ID should be reconciled according to the recipient’s instructions. Supporting proof may be required when the correction itself concerns the signer’s name.

Witnesses are not automatically required. A particular form or transaction may request witnesses, additional parties, or a specific notarial certificate. Confirm every participant before booking. The online witness guide explains the difference between the notary and document witnesses.

Remote online notarization may be available if the form, signer, witnesses, applicable rules, platform, and receiving party allow it. A recording office, DMV, title company, court, or bank may impose format or delivery requirements beyond notarization.

Acceptance Considerations and Common Mistakes

The record custodian or receiving party decides whether to accept and apply the correction. A notarized affidavit does not itself amend a public record, vehicle title, deed, contract, court file, vital record, or agency database. The recipient must process it under its rules.

Common mistakes include correcting the wrong version, failing to identify the original record, changing substantive terms under the label of a clerical correction, using a generic form instead of an agency form, omitting required parties, signing too early, providing inconsistent evidence, or assuming an electronic notarization is automatically recordable.

If the correction could affect ownership, money, inheritance, liens, legal rights, litigation, or a government benefit, seek qualified advice before notarization. SafeNotarize does not assess the legal effect of the proposed correction.

Affidavit of Correction FAQ

Practical answers about document preparation, participants, and online notarization.

What kinds of errors may a correction affidavit address?

It may be used for certain factual or clerical errors when the receiving organization permits it. The recipient decides whether the error requires an affidavit, amendment, replacement document, or another process.

Can a correction affidavit change the parties to a contract?

Do not assume so. A change to parties, obligations, ownership, price, or other substantive terms may require a new agreement or legal process rather than a correction affidavit.

Who should sign an affidavit correcting a document error?

The proper affiant depends on the original document, source of the error, personal knowledge, and recipient rules. Ask the custodian or qualified adviser who must sign.

Does the original document need to be attached?

Some recipients require a copy, original, recording reference, or supporting evidence; others prohibit attachments containing sensitive information. Follow the recipient’s checklist.

Can an affidavit correct a vehicle title or registration record?

A motor-vehicle agency may have a specific correction form and procedure. Use the agency’s current instructions rather than assuming a general affidavit is sufficient.

Can a recorded deed be changed with a notarized correction statement?

Recording rules vary, and substantive deed changes may require more than an affidavit. Contact the recorder, title company, or qualified legal professional before proceeding.

Will SafeNotarize review whether the correction is legally valid?

No. SafeNotarize performs the notarial act and does not evaluate the legal effect, sufficiency, or proper correction method.

What happens after the correction affidavit is notarized?

Submit it according to the receiving organization’s instructions. The custodian decides whether to accept it, request more evidence, record it, or require another document.
