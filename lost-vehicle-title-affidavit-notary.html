What a Lost Vehicle Title Affidavit Is

A lost vehicle title affidavit or sworn statement describes facts about a missing certificate of title. Depending on the jurisdiction and transaction, it may state that the paper title was lost, destroyed, stolen, never delivered, or lost in transit. It may identify the vehicle, titled owner, lienholder, and circumstances of the loss.

The affidavit is usually supporting evidence, not the replacement title itself. Florida maintains a separate procedure for duplicate and lost-in-transit certificates of title, and the customer may need an official application, identification, fees, lien information, ownership proof, or authorization. Other states use different forms.

SafeNotarize can notarize an eligible completed affidavit requested by the title agency or receiving organization. We do not issue duplicate titles, confirm title status, search ownership records, report a theft, or decide whether an affidavit is required.

Common Situations and Important Distinctions

A titled owner may discover that the paper certificate cannot be found before a sale, gift, move, refinancing, lien release, estate transfer, or registration transaction. A title might also have been mailed to the wrong address, damaged in a disaster, held by a lender, converted to electronic form, or lost after a prior transaction.

These situations are not identical. A duplicate title for a missing certificate differs from a title that was never transferred, a lost-in-transit title, an electronic title, a lienholder-held title, a title with incorrect information, or a vehicle for which ownership cannot be established. The agency may route each through a different procedure.

If the title exists but contains an error, review the Vehicle Title Affidavit guide and Affidavit of Correction guide. Do not characterize a disputed ownership or missing chain of title as a simple lost document.

Who Signs and What Facts Should Be Ready

The registered or titled owner commonly signs a duplicate-title application or supporting lost-title statement. A lienholder, company officer, trustee, personal representative, attorney-in-fact, or other representative may sign in some cases if the agency accepts the authority and supporting documents.

Prepare the owner’s exact name and address, vehicle identification number, year, make, body type or model, title number if available, license plate, lienholder information, date or approximate period of loss, and a truthful description of what happened. If the title was stolen, the agency may ask for a police report or theft information.

When multiple owners appear on the title, the connector between the names and the agency’s signature rules can affect who must participate. A notary does not interpret the title record or decide whether one owner may act alone. Ask the issuing agency which owners and representatives must sign.

Identification, Notarization, and Online Eligibility

The signer must present acceptable government-issued photo identification and complete the remote identity process. The identification name should match or be reconciled with the titled-owner name. If the owner’s name has changed, the agency may request marriage, divorce, court, or other supporting records.

Some duplicate-title applications or affidavits may require notarization, while others use agency certification language or permit alternative submission methods. Remote notarization is available only when the document, notarial certificate, signer, platform, and receiving agency support it.

Before booking, ask whether the title office accepts an electronic signature, remote online notarization, a printed copy of the electronic record, and mailed or electronic submission. A notary cannot certify a photocopy as the title, replace a missing state-issued certificate, or guarantee that the application will be approved.

Liens, Powers of Attorney, and Sales

A lien can affect who holds the title and who may request a duplicate. A paper certificate may not actually be lost if it is held by a lender or maintained electronically. Verify lien status before signing a sworn statement that the title is missing.

If another person will handle the duplicate-title request or sale, the agency may require a specific motor-vehicle power of attorney. Review the Vehicle Power of Attorney guide and confirm the form with the title office. A general letter of authorization may not be sufficient.

Do not complete a sale based only on a lost-title affidavit unless the title agency and transaction professionals approve the process. A Vehicle Bill of Sale documents sale facts but does not by itself replace a title. A Vehicle Gift Affidavit similarly does not create an official certificate.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using a generic lost-document affidavit instead of the issuing agency’s duplicate-title application.
  • Assuming an electronic title is lost because no paper certificate was received.
  • Failing to check whether a lienholder possesses or controls the title.
  • Entering an incorrect VIN, title number, owner name, or address.
  • Leaving out another titled owner or required representative.
  • Calling a disputed or never-transferred title a simple lost title.
  • Signing a theft statement without making any required report.
  • Expecting notarization to issue a duplicate title or authorize an immediate sale.

Use the agency checklist and current form. Keep copies of the completed application, affidavit, payment receipt, tracking information, and any correspondence.

Official Duplicate-Title Procedures Control

The Florida Motor Vehicle Procedures Manual lists a dedicated procedure for duplicate and lost-in-transit certificates of title, separate from corrections, transfers, identity affidavits, and powers of attorney. Follow the current FLHSMV or county tax collector instructions for the exact title situation.

When the vehicle is titled elsewhere, contact that issuing jurisdiction. Confirm the application, affidavit, fees, lien documents, owner signatures, identification, electronic-notarization acceptance, and submission channel before the session. The receiving agency—not SafeNotarize—decides whether the evidence is sufficient.

Lost Vehicle Title Affidavit FAQ

Answers to common preparation and online-notarization questions.

Does a lost title affidavit give me a new vehicle title?

No. An affidavit may support a duplicate-title request, but only the issuing motor vehicle agency can approve the application and issue the replacement certificate or electronic title.

What if my Florida title is electronic rather than lost?

An electronic title is maintained in the state system and may require a paper-title request or another service rather than a lost-title affidavit. Check the title status before applying.

Can one co-owner apply for a duplicate title alone?

That depends on how the owners are listed and the agency’s signature rules. Ask the title office which owners must sign and whether a representative may act.

Will a bill of sale replace the missing title?

A bill of sale records transaction facts but generally does not replace an official title. The buyer and seller should follow the issuing agency’s lost-title and transfer procedures.

Can a lienholder request the replacement title?

A lienholder may have rights or responsibilities in the title process, but the exact procedure depends on the title record and jurisdiction. Confirm directly with the agency and lienholder.

What should I do if I believe the title was stolen?

Contact the motor vehicle agency and appropriate law-enforcement office for reporting instructions. Do not rely only on notarization, and keep any report number or written guidance.

Can I notarize a lost-title statement while living in another country?

An eligible signer may be able to use remote notarization, but the issuing title authority must accept the document, identity process, electronic record, and delivery method.

How long does a duplicate-title request take after notarization?

Processing time is controlled by the agency, service center, mail, title status, and completeness of the submission. SafeNotarize does not issue the title or control processing time.
